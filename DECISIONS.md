# Design Decisions Log

Format: `YYYY-MM-DD — decision — who — why`

- 2026-10-04 — Three servicer models (`ff_small/medium/large`) share one arm via `models/common/`; only base size and mass differ (20 / 150 / 1500 kg → arm/base ratio 0.33 / 0.044 / 0.0044) — P1 — the mass-ratio sweep must vary one thing; one arm definition prevents the three files drifting apart.
- 2026-10-04 — Shoulder mounted on the base +x face, so the shoulder offset from the base CoM scales with base size (0.2 / 0.4 / 0.8 m) — P1 — with a fixed offset the large base swallowed the arm and the small base left it floating; a real servicer mounts its arm on its hull.
- 2026-10-04 — Joint actuators are velocity servos (`ctrl` = commanded qdot, kv = 20, ±5 Nm torque limit), replacing torque motors — P1 — all five schemes output qdot. Actuator torques are internal, so momentum is still conserved and the base reacts through the real dynamics, which writing `qvel` directly would bypass. **Provisional: P2/P3 to confirm.**
- 2026-10-04 — Integrator RK4 at 0.25 ms (was implicitfast at 2 ms) — P1 — measured worst-case total-momentum drift over 10 s of random qdot steps: implicitfast/Euler @ 2 ms ~1e-3 (first-order, fails the 1e-6 gate); RK4 @ 0.5 ms, kv 10 ~6e-7 (marginal); RK4 @ 0.25 ms, kv 20 ~1.2e-7 (10× margin). Runs ~9× faster than real time.
- 2026-10-04 — Antenna site on the base top face; its z-axis is the boresight used for the ±5° pointing limit (R1) and the demo-video cone — P1.
- 2026-10-04 — `pygame` replaced by `pygame-ce` — P1 — pygame 2.6.1's font module fails to import on Python 3.14 (circular import), so no on-screen text; pygame-ce is the maintained drop-in fork (`import pygame` unchanged).
- 2026-10-04 — Teleop: own pygame window rather than the MuJoCo viewer — P1 — the viewer's own key shortcuts (W, S, A, D, …) clash with WASD input; pygame also reads the gamepad later and lets us draw the antenna lines and HUD; see the rendering entry below.
- 2026-10-04 — Teleop rates: control 100 Hz (command held over 40 physics steps), display ~33 fps; operator twist expressed in the world frame with a world-fixed camera — P1 — world frame keeps base drift visible on screen. **Provisional: P5 to confirm for the protocol.**
- 2026-10-04 — Controller interface `controller(model, data, xdot, mode) -> qdot`, registered as `SCHEMES` in `src/schemes.py`; the loop scales qdot uniformly into ±1 rad/s — P1 — so P3's schemes plug in without touching teleop. **Provisional: P3 to confirm.**
- 2026-10-04 — Teleop window is a pygame OpenGL window that MuJoCo draws into directly (`MjrContext` + `mjr_render`, text via `mjr_overlay`), not off-screen rendering blitted into pygame — P1 — on a hybrid NVIDIA/AMD GNOME-Wayland laptop the off-screen path (GLFW or EGL) gave a black window while direct rendering works (tested with a 3-stage display diagnostic).
- 2026-10-04 — `home` keyframe (elbow/wrist bent) in `models/common/arm_defs.xml`; teleop starts and resets from it — P1 — the all-zero pose is a fully straight arm, a singularity where the hand cannot move along the arm. `--controller demo` (fixed-base DLS follower) added for showing the base reaction; it is not a benchmark scheme.
- 2026-10-04 — Joint limits (±170° roll/yaw, ±120° pitch; generic values, no specific arm) and collisions (arm–base, arm–self; shoulder link excluded from the base) turned on — P1 — without them the arm swept a full sphere through the base, which no real arm can. Solver tolerance 1e-12 and soft constraints (solref 0.1 s) keep momentum drift < 1e-7 when hitting limits/base (default settings gave 1.6e-6, over the gate); cost ≤ ~1° past a limit / ~5 mm into the base at full speed.
- 2026-10-04 — Reachable workspace (`src/workspace.py`, V in teleop): 30k random within-limit, collision-free joint samples, binned into 10 cm surface cubes — P1 — shows the base cutting the workspace (small base: near-full sphere; large base: half-dome).
- 2026-10-04 — `home` pose changed to shoulder −50°, elbow 100°, wrist −50° (hand level, ~85% reach, 20° elbow margin) — P1 — the first home pose sat at 97% reach and then 10° from the elbow limit, so task waypoints were unreachable.
- 2026-10-04 — Task: `start → approach → ORU → approach → stow → start`, 2 loops; waypoint reached when the actual hand stays within 2 cm (+10° in 6-D) for 0.3 s; waypoints fixed in the world frame; scripted mode follows a minimum-jerk reference at teleop full speed — P1 — the plan names a "closed-loop ORU task" but no geometry. Demo controller completes it on medium/large; on small it fails because it ignores base motion (expected). **Provisional: P5 to confirm for the protocol.**
- 2026-10-04 — Session: 5 schemes × 2 modes on medium; modes as blocks alternating by participant parity; scheme order = Williams Latin square row (10 rows for n = 5), second block 5 rows further; controller hidden from participant; Backspace disabled during trials; scheme keys `dls, grad_proj, impedance, rns, impedance_rns` — P1 — plan Week 8 study design. **Provisional: P5 (design) and P3 (key names) to confirm.**
- 2026-10-14 — `src/dynamics.py` ported from `v1/src/dynamics.py` to the root models (site `end_effector`, `mj_fullM`); maths unchanged: J* = J_m + J_b V, V = −H_b⁻¹H_bm, reactionless = W q̇ = 0 with W = V[3:] (not N(H_bm,ang), v1 D3) — P3 for P2 — the root schemes and the momentum gate need it. Measured: momentum residual ≤ 8.5e-8 over 1 s random q̇ on all three servicers; J* and W predict hand twist / base rate to ≤ 2e-9; RNS is 4-D. **P2 to review.**
- 2026-10-14 — `tests/test_momentum.py` filled in (the Week 6 gate): random q̇ for 10 s, residual checked every physics step, all three servicers, < 1e-6; plus J*/W prediction and the DOF-count check (rank [J*; W] = 7 < 9 rows in 6-D, = 6 in 3-D) — P3 for P2.
- 2026-10-14 — Schemes interface: `SCHEMES` holds `Scheme` objects, still callable as `controller(model, data, xdot, mode)`. Teleop passes the commanded target (blue sphere) as `target=` when the controller has `accepts_target`; every scheme then servos onto it with the same gain, xdot += K_POSE (target − hand), K_POSE = 2 /s, clipped at 5 cm / 0.3 rad — P3 — pure rate control drifts away from the waypoints (DLS damping, the 6-D trade-off) and the scripted gate could not finish; this is the same pull P1's `demo` uses. Same for all five, so the comparison stays fair. **P1 / P5 to confirm.**
- 2026-10-14 — Joint geofence on all five schemes: a joint within 3° of its limit and still pushing out is locked and the rest re-solve (v1 D13 idea) — P3 — keeps the arm off the soft limit stops; applied to all schemes equally.
- 2026-10-14 — Gains (P3, starting from v1 D5, checked by `python -m src.schemes --gate`): λ = 0.02, KG = 5, KP = 1, KD = 0.1, KA = 5 (3-D), TW = 100 (3-D task-row weight), γ = 1e-3, β = 3, KA6 = 1 (6-D). 6-D choice from a scripted KA6 × β grid on medium (2 loops, timeout 1.5× reference): KA6 = 5 with β ≥ 1 leaves the hand stuck 2–5 cm short of a waypoint (the attitude pull fights the pose servo), so a participant could never finish; KA6 = 0 (bare plan formula, β = 10) finishes but ends with more base drift than DLS (0.72° vs 0.30°). β = 3, KA6 = 1 is the most reactionless setting that finishes for both 4 and 5: peak / final antenna error RNS 1.59° / 0.24°, Imp.+RNS 1.42° / 0.17°, DLS 1.78° / 0.30°; rms hand error 8–9 mm vs 1 mm. γ = 1e-2 fought the task in 6-D (14 mm vs 5 mm rms at β = 0.3). **Re-tune per scheme in the recess (no strawmen) and log here.**
- 2026-10-14 — 3-D RNS solve kept singularity-robust: the stacked [TW·J*; W] is damped (EPS = 1e-6) without rescaling W, which is ~100× smaller than J*, so the reactionless rows soften where the stack loses rank. Rescaling W to unit rows made it exact (W q̇ ≈ 0) but 3-D 4 and 5 then saturated at 1 rad/s and failed the loop on all three servicers (22/30). The damped form leaves ~25× less base rate than DLS at a test pose and 0.002–0.035° final base error over 2 loops on medium / large — P3.
- 2026-10-14 — Final gate (`python -m src.schemes --gate --procs 8`), all 5 schemes × 2 modes × 3 servicers, 26/30 complete, all stable: large 10/10 and medium 10/10. Medium final antenna error, 3-D: DLS 0.041°, Grad 1.07°, Imp 0.036°, RNS 0.002°, Imp.+RNS 0.003°; 6-D: DLS 0.295°, Grad 0.265°, Imp 0.240°, RNS 0.238°, Imp.+RNS 0.171°. Small: 3-D RNS and Imp.+RNS do not finish (exact reactionless reach on the 20 kg base is too short for the ±15 cm loop, as v1 D14 found ~5 cm) and 6-D RNS / Imp.+RNS do not finish; 6-D DLS ends 12° off and Impedance 8° — over the ±5° antenna limit. That is the Fig. 2 result (drift matters on the small servicer), not a bug. Grad. proj. drifts the posture most (|q_f − q_0| up to 1.6 rad) as expected from maximising manipulability. — P3.
- 2026-10-14 — `src/safety.py` first version (P3 for P4): Λ(q) = (J M⁻¹ Jᵀ)⁻¹ with the full floating M; m_eff along u; joints locked (default, conservative) or free; impulse, Δv of an unrestrained body, the plan's v_max = limit / ((1+e) m_eff), and ISO/TS 15066's energy model v = F_max / √(μ k) for the map. Home pose, tool axis, chest: m_eff 3.2 / 10.7 / 155 kg, v_max 1.02 / 0.61 / 0.31 m/s (small / medium / large, unrestrained) — the bigger base hits harder. Teleop HUD shows hand speed against v_max along the direction of travel. **P4: the body values (chest 280 N, 25 N/mm, 40 kg; hand 280 N, 75 N/mm, 0.6 kg) are recalled from ISO/TS 15066 Annex A and must be checked against the standard; e = 0 needs a justification.**
- 2026-10-14 — PS4 pad in `src/teleop.py` (P3 for P1, README said it would be added): mirrors the keyboard (left stick x/y, R2/L2 z, R1/L1 roll, right stick pitch/yaw, Square = fine, Cross = start, Circle = snap target, Triangle = workspace, Options = pause, Share = reset), works alongside the keyboard, hot-plug, rumbles when the antenna leaves the ±5° cone. SDL2 numbering checked on a real DualShock 4 (Windows) with `pad_check.py`. Untouched triggers read 0 in SDL (= half pressed) and are treated as released. **P1 / P5 to confirm the mapping for the protocol.**
- 2026-10-14 — PS4 default changed to a game-style layout (`--pad-layout game`): left stick moves the target relative to the camera (up = away from you on screen), R2/L2 world up/down, right stick orbits the camera (90°/s), hold R1 = rotate mode (stick tilts/turns the tool flight-stick style, R2/L2 roll), hold L1 (or Square) = fine, D-pad zoom, R3 / Home resets the view. `southpaw` swaps the sticks; `classic` keeps the world-axis, fixed-camera mapping. Camera az/el/distance logged as `cam_0..2` (NaN headless) and the layout goes in the session `#` header — P3 — classmates know the game convention, so less learning and less noise in the predictability / NASA-TLX ratings; a turnable view helps judge depth. Costs: a 7th task-irrelevant input to learn and camera time in the trial, and the view now differs between participants (base drift is still visible because the camera orbits a world-fixed point, not the base). The keyboard stays in world axes. **P5: choose ONE layout for the whole study, pilot it, and confirm against the scope freeze; P1: review the loop changes.**
- 2026-10-14 — Haptics on the PS4 pad (`src/teleop.py haptic_signals`, on by default, `--no-haptics` to switch off): heavy motor = the arm touching the base or itself, 0.35 at first touch up to 1.0 at 10 N total contact force (`mj_contactForce`); light motor = the joint nearest its limit, ramping 0.25 → 1.0 over the last 15°; a 250 ms both-motor pulse when the antenna leaves the ±5° cone (wins over the continuous buzz). Red dots mark contact points; the HUD shows contact force and the nearest-limit joint for every participant (it does not reveal the scheme). Logged per tick: `haptic_0/1`, `contact_force`, `joint_margin_deg`; `haptics` in the session header — P3 — the operator otherwise only finds a collision or a locked joint by watching. Measured: driving every joint at full speed, the 15° zone is reached in ~0.1 s and the arm hits the base after ~0.9 s (medium, 24 N at first touch, peak ~65 N); a normal scripted ORU loop (Imp.+RNS and Grad. proj., medium, 6-D) never triggers either motor, so the buzz only means something is wrong. Both motors confirmed on the real DualShock 4 (`rumble()` returns True). **P5: haptics is an extra feedback channel — keep it identical for every scheme and participant, and say so in the protocol; P4: candidate mitigation row for the hazard table (operator warning of contact / limit).**
